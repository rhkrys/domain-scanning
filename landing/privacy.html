3.The Domain and Website Security Scanner

The Domain and Website Security Scanner (the "Scanner") reads public records about a website or email domain, such as DNS, email authentication (SPF, DKIM, DMARC), SSL/TLS, and HTTP security headers. It also reads the public response from the website's home page for signs of the software it runs, such as PHP and its version. It turns the results into a grade and a list of suggested next steps.

  • Authorization. Only scan domains you own or have permission to assess. By running a scan, you confirm you have that authority.
  • A starting point, not an audit. A scan is a point-in-time check of public information. It is not a full security audit, penetration test, or compliance assessment, and it can miss problems.
  • Passive only. The Scanner makes the same kind of request a web browser makes. It does not send attacks, test for vulnerabilities by trying to exploit them, or change anything on your site.
  • Software results are a best guess. Many sites hide their software version, and some hosts fix older version numbers without changing them. A software result tells you what to confirm with your host or web person. It does not confirm your site is patched or unpatched.
  • No guarantee. A good grade does not mean a domain is secure, and a poor grade does not mean it has been compromised. Decisions you make based on a report are your responsibility.
  • Fair use. We may rate-limit or block scans that we believe are abusive, automated, or aimed at domains you do not control.
