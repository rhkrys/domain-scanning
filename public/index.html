    What we check

    The scan runs 12 checks in three groups. It only reads public information about your domain, so nothing on your domain changes.

    Domain setup

    • Mail servers (MX). Shows where your email is delivered, so messages reach the right inbox.
    • Website address (A and AAAA). Confirms your domain points to your website, including the newer IPv6 format.
    • Nameservers. Checks you have at least two, so one failure does not take your domain offline.
    • DNSSEC. Checks whether your DNS answers are digitally signed, so visitors reach your real site.
    • CAA. Checks whether you have limited which companies can issue certificates for your domain.

    Email protection

    • SPF. Lists which servers may send email as your domain, and how strict that list is.
    • DMARC. Tells inboxes what to do with mail that fails checks, and where to send reports.
    • DKIM. Adds a signature to your outgoing mail so receivers can see it really came from you.
    • MTA-STS. Looks for a policy that asks other mail servers to send to you over an encrypted connection.
    • BIMI. An optional record that can show your logo next to your email in supporting inboxes.

    Website encryption

    • TLS certificate. Checks your site’s certificate is valid and not close to expiring, so browsers connect without warnings.
    • HTTP security headers. Looks for five settings that tell browsers how to protect your visitors, such as HSTS and Content-Security-Policy.

    Two limits to know. We look for the most common DKIM selector names, so a custom setup can be missed. For MTA-STS we look for the published record, not the policy file behind it. Your mail provider, or a call with us, can confirm both.

    What a good checker should show you

    Whichever tool you use, look for these six things in the results.

    • Record found. A DMARC record exists on your domain.
    • One record only. Two DMARC records at the same name cause DMARC to be ignored.
    • Valid syntax. The record is written correctly, so receivers can read it.
    • Policy level. Whether the policy is none, quarantine or reject.
    • Reporting set up. A reporting address (rua) so you receive reports about who sends as your domain.
    • SPF and DKIM alignment. A pass only counts when the checked domain matches your From domain.

    The Tech in Peace scan reads your DMARC record, its policy level and its reporting address. Checking for duplicate records, syntax and alignment, and fixing them, is hands-on work we do with you on a call.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is the scan free?

    Yes. Enter your domain and your results appear on screen at no cost. Add your email if you want the report sent to you. You do not need an account.

    Do I need SPF and DKIM before DMARC?

    You can publish DMARC first, but mail only passes DMARC when SPF or DKIM passes and matches your From domain. Most domains set up both. A common start is DMARC at p=none to collect reports, then tighten the policy.

    Why is DMARC failing when SPF passes?

    DMARC needs the SPF result to match your From domain. SPF checks a hidden sending address that can belong to a different domain, such as your email service. A matching DKIM signature can still make DMARC pass.

    What does p=none mean?

    p=none is monitor mode. It asks receivers to take no special action on mail that fails DMARC, so you can collect reports first. It does not ask them to block spoofed mail. The usual next steps are quarantine, then reject, once reports look clean.

    How often should I scan my domain?

    Scan after any change to your email provider, website host or DNS, and then every few months. Adding a new sending service is a common reason SPF or DKIM stops matching.

    What happens after I scan?

    You see a grade, a score and a ranked list of fixes on screen. If you add your email, we send the same report. The scan only reads public information, so nothing changes on your domain. You can book a call if you want help with the fixes.

    What does DMARC alignment mean?

    Alignment means the domain that passed SPF or DKIM matches the domain in your From address. DMARC passes when at least one of them passes and aligns. This stops a message from borrowing a pass from an unrelated domain.
